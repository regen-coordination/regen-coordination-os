## Gate results (§5)

Palette was measured, not eyeballed — hue-histogram against the `DESIGN.md` §2 sky-to-sun ramp,
counting pink/lilac/violet pixels as drift. **The documented pastel-drift failure did not occur in
this batch:** drift ≤ 0.6% everywhere, versus 45–80% of pixels sitting in brand hues. An initial
visual read wrongly flagged two candidates as "dusty rose"; those bands measure hue ≈ 25°, inside
the `#e0b789` range. Grain was measured as high-frequency residual against the references.

**The single discard** is `2-network-3`, on the prompt-2 gate *"are the tiers actually stacked and
legible, not one cloud?"* It is the prettiest of the batch and the closest match to RC's shipped
artifact — which is exactly the problem. It reproduces RC's undifferentiated lattice, so a Toolkit
entry carrying it would be visually indistinguishable from the RC entry, losing the one thing
prompt 2 exists to show. A stack, not a cloud.

Batch 2 carries visibly less grain than its reference (2.0–2.5 vs 3.95). Not disqualifying — grain
is present — but batch 1 sits at 4.7–5.1 against a 4.81 reference, i.e. dead on.

## Why the winner

Batch 1 draft 2, mastered at pro/2K. The master passed the winning draft back in as a reference
rather than regenerating from text alone, so it tracks the chosen composition; of the two masters,
**#2** matched the draft's profile (79.9% brand, grain 4.37) while #1 drifted greener and flatter
(68.6%, 3.30).

It wins on the prompt-1 gate: the strata read as genuinely distinct bands, and the network crosses
every one of them with visible nodes where filaments meet — the Toolkit's architecture, not an
undifferentiated blob. Prompt 1 was the handoff's PRIMARY on the argument that a cross-section is
what the Toolkit *is*; the renders bear that out.
